History · 2014

An evening ceremony that grew into a three-day gathering.

The 2014 Vishnupuram Literary Circle Award was presented to the poet Gnanakkoothan in Coimbatore, at a gathering that stretched from a single evening into a three-day meeting of writers and readers — translated and adapted from the original Tamil account.

The recipient

Gnanakkoothan, the poet, was named the recipient of the Vishnupuram Literary Circle Award for 2014, carrying with it a cash award of one lakh rupees. The decision was settled by the Circle in August of that year, with the ceremony itself following at the end of December.

A gathering that outgrew an evening

What was planned as an award ceremony turned, in the organising, into something larger. Friends of the Circle in Coimbatore — led by V. Suresh, with Selvendran, Vijay Suryan, Sivakumar (known to friends as “Steel” Siva), Ravi (“Shimoga”), Radhakrishnan (“Tambi”), Senthil (“Kvis”) and Thiagu of the Thiagu Book Centre — put together a three-day meeting running from 27 to 29 December 2014, with Arangasamy managing the proceedings from day to day. More than two hundred people, by the article's account, moved through the gathering over the three days, held between the Rajasthani Nivas guest house and a nearby marriage hall.

Talks, arguments, and a late arrival

The days were filled with talks rather than ceremony. Pavannan spoke about currents in Kannada writing and the Dalit literature movement, drawing as well on his own long years in BSNL service. Pooyarasu traced how the Vaanam Paadi poetry movement had turned verse into an instrument of social argument. Su. Venugopal spoke about his own writing, particularly his attempts to hold on to the texture of agrarian life as Tamil villages changed around him. T.P. Rajeevan took part in a running debate on where contemporary poetry stood — between image-making and a more argumentative, reasoned kind of verse. Writers including Nanchil Nadan, Sa. Kandasamy, Devadevan, K. Mohanrangan and M. Gopalakrishnan moved among the readers throughout, and the filmmaker Vasanthabalan joined discussions on the roots of Tamil and Indian culture. The artist Shanmugavel, an illustrator known for his work on Jeyamohan's Venmurasu, was also among those present.

Gnanakkoothan himself arrived later than planned — a flight delay held him back until the afternoon of the 27th, rather than the morning the organisers had expected — but he reached Coimbatore in time to join the evening's proceedings.

A book and a film

M. A. Susila's autobiography, Yaathumaagi, was released at the gathering. Before the formal ceremony, a forty-minute documentary on Gnanakkoothan's life and writing, Ilai Mel Ezhuthu (“Writing on a Leaf”), made by the filmmaker K. P. Vinodh, was screened for the assembled crowd.

The ceremony

On the evening of 27 December, the gathering moved to the marriage hall for the formal presentation of the award, beginning at six and running until nine. The article does not record the particulars of who read the citation or handed over the trophy that evening, nor does it describe remarks from Gnanakkoothan himself at the ceremony — its attention is given instead to the three days of talk and argument that surrounded it.

What the account does note is the mood of the crowd: across the long sessions, no one seemed to tire, and more than a few people were seen taking notes as the writers spoke.

Five years on

By 2014 the Vishnupuram Literary Circle Award was five years old, having begun in 2009 out of an informal conversation among friends who wanted a prize given by readers and writers themselves — by people who had actually read the work of those they honoured, closely and over time. The Coimbatore gathering, grown well beyond a single evening's ceremony, was itself a sign of how that idea had taken root.
