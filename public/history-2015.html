History · 2015

A long day of poetry talk, honors, and a stubborn microphone in Coimbatore.

The 2015 Vishnupuram Literary Circle Award went to the poet Devathaasan, celebrated at a day-long gathering in Coimbatore that mixed serious literary discussion with music, tributes, and a handful of technical mishaps — translated and adapted from the original Tamil account.

The recipient

Devathaasan, the poet, received the Vishnupuram Literary Circle Award for 2015. His work had already drawn serious critical attention within the Circle — a companion essay by Jeyamohan published around the same time examined how Devathaasan's poetry finds intimations of the infinite inside the smallest, most ordinary moments, situating him within a lineage of modern Tamil poets who moved away from classical formality and Romantic transcendence toward truths discovered in daily life.

A morning devoted to his poetry

The ceremony was held on 27 December 2015, at the auditorium of a school in Coimbatore. The gathering had begun the night before with singing that ran past midnight, and participants were up again by half past five the next morning to continue.

The morning session turned to a close reading of Devathaasan's poetry, with the poet Isai, the writers Lakshmi Manivannan, K. N. Senthil and M. Gopalkrishnan, and the critic K. Mohanrangan taking part. Two recurring concerns ran through the discussion: Tamil criticism's lack of an accessible vocabulary for talking about poetry outside small specialist circles, and the inconsistent way poets themselves used critical terms.

An afternoon argument about history and fiction

In the afternoon, the writer Murugavel — author of the novel Milirgal and translator of Eriyum Panikkaadu — spoke about his work, with Suresh, Vijayaraghavan and Gopalkrishnan responding. The exchange turned into a pointed debate on whether history could ever be objective: Lakshmi Manivannan argued that all history is essentially a form of fiction, while Murugavel maintained that an objective reality exists independently of however it gets interpreted.

The evening ceremony

The formal proceedings began around 5:30 in the evening, opening with a devotional song performed by Vanathi, Suresh's daughter. The hall's sound system failed early on and had to be hastily patched together, and outdated projection equipment delayed a documentary screening later in the program — small disruptions that the organizers worked around as the evening continued.

Christopher's novel Thurayvan was released during the ceremony by the writer Joe D. Cruz, with V. Alex receiving the first copy. The illustrator Shanmugavel, known for his work on Venmurasu, was honored with a shawl and a cash award presented by Rajagopal and Vijayaraghavan, with Senthil handing over the prize. Devathaasan and his wife were then honored together with shawls and garlands.

A separate award for the writer Lenin Rajendran, who could not attend because of illness, was accepted on his behalf by Nanjanadan. A documentary about Devathaasan's life and work, titled Nisapthathin Saptham, was released on disc by the director Vetrimaaran, with Kadaloore Seenu receiving the copy.

A critical study, and closing words

Alongside the tributes, a critical study of Devathaasan's poetry, Athuvaana Valiyil Kavithai (roughly, "Poetry in the Boundless Path"), brought out by Solvanam Press, was released by the writer Yuvan Chandrasekhar and received by Vijay Suryan.

Speaking about Devathaasan's work, Yuvan Chandrasekhar pointed to a line — "wait, without hurrying" — that struck him first as merely funny, before it slowly taught him something about patience and staying inside the present moment. Lakshmi Manivannan described her own first encounter with the poetry as something closer to a physical shift, as though an organ inside her had quietly moved to a new place. One of the speakers summed up the poet's larger project simply: poetry, he said, is what places a child, or someone from a tribal community, back at the center of civilization.

Suresh closed the formal program with a vote of thanks. The literary conversation, though, did not really end there — the gathering carried on informally over dinner at a Rajasthani restaurant before everyone finally went their separate ways.
