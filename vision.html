Vision

A readers' movement, now honouring the world.

Living Tamil emerged from the Vishnupuram Literary Circle — sixteen years of readers building a serious literary culture in Tamil — and from Jeyamohan's meetings with a young diaspora hungry for the tradition they had almost lost.

The parent circle

The Vishnupuram Literary Circle was founded on 26 January 2010 in Coimbatore by the readers and friends of the Tamil writer Jeyamohan. It is named for his novel Vishnupuram — a name chosen to say plainly that the circle belongs to no city and no faction, only to readers.

In the years since, it has grown into a global network: chapters in the United States (2021), in Europe based in Vienna (2025), in the United Kingdom (2026), and roughly twenty-five informal monthly gatherings across the world. Its awards programme has honoured Tamil writers every year since 2010. Its online encyclopedia — Tamil Wiki — has grown into one of the largest freely-read reference works in an Indian language.

The spark

In 2025, the American publisher Farrar, Straus and Giroux released Jeyamohan's Stories of the True — the first Tamil short-story collection to appear from a major US literary imprint. On his coast-to-coast tour, Jeyamohan met the young Tamil diaspora: readers who could not read Tamil, but who wanted to know what their parents had carried across the ocean and could not fully pass on.

A literary festival inspired by Jeyamohan's readers seeks to introduce diaspora youth to Tamil fiction, folklore, and history. Frontline · March 2026

From those meetings came the Living Tamil LitFest, held in New York on 3–4 April 2026 with over fifty writers, scholars and translators — and, in the same season, this: the Living Tamil Award for World Literature.

Why this award

Most literary prizes reward a single book, in a single year, in a single language. A writer's true gift is the shape of a lifetime — the way a body of work, taken together, changes how a language reads itself and how the world reads that language. Living Tamil looks at that longer arc, and reaches across languages.

  • Any language. Open to writers in every literary tradition. Translation is a bridge, not a barrier.
  • Any geography. The award follows the writing, not the passport.
  • A lifetime, not a season. We honour the arc of a career, not the fashion of a year.
  • An organised readership. The award is chosen and carried by a large, working network of serious readers on four continents.

An award born in India, addressed to the world

Living Tamil is the largest literary award ever to originate from India, and among the very few international prizes for lifetime achievement in literature run entirely by readers rather than a state or a publisher. It is our answer to a simple question: what would happen if a great readers' tradition looked outward, and offered its highest honour to the world?
